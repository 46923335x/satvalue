What is kS?

kS is a practical unit of account for expressing conventional asset prices in Bitcoin.

Prices are displayed in kS (“Kays”), where 1 kS = 1,000 satoshis.

One Bitcoin contains 100,000 kS. This intermediate unit avoids both tiny BTC decimals and unnecessarily large satoshi values.

Converting an asset price into Bitcoin

Converting a dollar price into Bitcoin works like any other currency conversion. Let PUSD be the asset price in dollars and PBTC its price in Bitcoin.

If the exchange rate is expressed as BTC per USD, multiply by that rate:

PBTC = PUSD × BTC per USD

Most markets quote Bitcoin as USD per BTC. With that familiar convention, divide instead:

PBTC = PUSDUSD/BTC

Converting Bitcoin into kS

Because 1 BTC equals 100,000 kS, multiply a BTC-denominated price by 100,000:

PkS = PBTC × 100,000

Combining the two conversions gives the formula SatValue uses:

PkS = PUSD × 100,000USD/BTC
A stock’s price in kS equals its dollar price multiplied by the current number of kS that one US dollar buys.
Stock price × kS per USD = stock price in kS

Example: Bitcoin at $200,000

At a Bitcoin price of $200,000, one US dollar buys 0.5 kS.

This is not a new valuation system. It is the same market price expressed in a different unit of account, just as a price can be converted from dollars into euros or yen.

Why kS?

A price such as 0.002358 BTC is awkward to scan, while 235,800 sats is unnecessarily large. The equivalent 235.8 kS is easier to read and compare.

Style guide

  • Symbol: kS
  • Spoken: “Kays”
  • Plural: “Kays”
  • Definition: 1 kS = 1,000 sats = 0.00001 BTC
